PC Storage Audit & File Organization

Taking care of the PC I built in 2024 through a storage audit, file organization, and a maintenance routine.

YEAR2026
RESULT231 GB recovered
ENVIRONMENTWindows 11 · Custom-built PC

[01] CONTEXT

Taking care of my own build

I built my PC in 2024, and I wanted to take better care of something I put together myself. Between school, projects, games, and downloads, quite a bit had accumulated. Some of it I still needed, and some I hadn’t touched in months.

I decided to go through what was taking up space, organize my files, and make it easier to maintain everything going forward. Since I’m building my IT portfolio, I thought this would also be a good opportunity to document my process and explain the decisions I made.

I started with 106 GB free and finished with 337 GB. Here’s how I recovered that space and gave my PC some overdue attention.

[02] ASSESSMENT

Start with a baseline

I exported a system report with msinfo32 before changing anything. My custom PC runs Windows 11 Home with an AMD Ryzen 5 5600G, 16 GB of RAM, and a 930 GB C: drive.

Storage Settings showed 106 GB free and 474 GB attributed to installed apps. That made unused applications my first area to review.

Run shortcuts: Win + R, then msinfo32 for System Information or ms-settings:storagesense for Storage Settings.

[03] CLEANUP

Decide what I actually need

I reviewed apps using ms-settings:appsfeatures and appwiz.cpl. I wasn’t expecting League of Legends to take up so much space at 35.7 GB. I hadn’t played in months, so I removed it along with Riot Client.

I also removed unused calendar, VPN, and virtualization software. I kept Genshin Impact’s game resources in case I want to return to it. This was a review of what I needed, rather than simply removing every large application.

Uninstalling apps brought free space to 146 GB. Reviewing and cleaning temporary files brought it to 148 GB. I then reviewed Downloads and removed unwanted files, reaching a checkpoint of 175 GB free.

I removed local OneDrive copies, emptied the Recycle Bin, and deleted large files I no longer wanted. The next screenshot showed 335 GB free. Those actions were not measured separately.

Finally, I removed Minecraft for Windows, the Bedrock Edition I don’t play. Its reported size was 2.35 GB, and my final reading was 337 GB free.

Run shortcut: shell:Downloads opens Downloads. Reported app sizes are estimates; I used drive free-space readings to measure the overall result.

[04] ORGANIZATION

Give the files I kept a home

I organized Documents into Career, Learning, Projects, Personal, and Archive folders, and tidied loose files on the Desktop. The goal was to make things easier to find and keep future project evidence together.

Moving files within C: improved organization but did not itself free storage.

Shortcuts: shell:Personal opens Documents. Ctrl + Shift + N creates a folder, Ctrl + X and Ctrl + V move files, and Win + D shows the Desktop.

[05] MAINTENANCE

Make the cleanup easier to maintain

I enabled Storage Sense and automatic temporary-file cleanup, with a monthly schedule and 30-day Recycle Bin retention. I left automatic Downloads cleanup and both cloud-content settings set to Never so those files stay under manual review.

Run shortcut: ms-settings:storagepolicies opens Storage Sense configuration.

I tested it manually after the cleanup. It removed another 10 MB. Most of the work had already been done, so that small result made sense. It was the result of one run, not the whole audit.

I confirmed that moved documents and regularly used applications opened normally, and that my OneDrive files were still accessible online. No cleanup errors were reported.

[07] REFLECTION

What I learned

Reviewing what I actually use helped me make better decisions about what to remove. I also learned to separate an app’s reported size from the space actually recovered, and a single Storage Sense run from the full cleanup result.

The file organization and monthly maintenance settings gave me a way to keep taking care of the PC after the audit was finished.
